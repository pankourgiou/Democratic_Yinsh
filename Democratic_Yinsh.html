Democratic Yinsh

Real Yinsh rules, but nobody decides alone. On your turn seven advisors propose actions and you vote from a ballot (or write one in). On the council's turn the same seven advisors debate and a voting program of your choice picks the move.

The council's debate appears here on its turn.

Yinsh, as implemented

  • Hexagonal board of 85 points. Each side places 5 rings, alternating.
  • Turn: choose a ring, a marker of your colour drops inside it, then the ring slides in a straight line over empty points. It may jump a run of markers (flipping each) and must stop on the first empty point after. It never crosses another ring.
  • Rows: five markers of one colour in a line are removed, then the owner removes one of their rings (+1 point). Rows made for the opponent score for them first.
  • Win at the chosen number of rings removed, or by points when all 51 markers are used. A side with no legal move passes.

The democratic layer

  • Every decision, even placing rings or picking which row to remove, goes to a vote.
  • Hawk chases rows, Guardian blocks, Surveyor wants mobility, Weaver builds links, Pioneer races for rings, Trickster is erratic, Pragmatist balances.
  • Stubbornness is how reluctant advisors are to change their minds during debate.
